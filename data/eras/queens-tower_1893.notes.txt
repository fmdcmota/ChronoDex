FACTS:
- Queen Victoria formally opened the Imperial Institute on 10 May 1893. (fun fact) [https://www.british-history.ac.uk/survey-london/vol38/pp220-227]
- The tower formed the centre of a roughly 700-foot frontage with smaller towers at both ends. [https://www.imperial.ac.uk/about/history/queens-tower/]
- The exterior used Portland Whitbed stone and contrasting brickwork. [https://www.british-history.ac.uk/survey-london/vol38/pp220-227]
- Imperial Institute Road was laid out south of the building in 1888–92. [https://www.british-history.ac.uk/survey-london/vol38/pp220-227]
- The central tower rose to 287 feet; its base walls were nine feet thick. (fun fact) [https://www.british-history.ac.uk/survey-london/vol38/pp220-227]
- The official opening used a temporary hall; the proposed great hall was never built. (fun fact) [https://www.british-history.ac.uk/survey-london/vol38/pp220-227]
- A billiard-room was added in 1893 to attract subscribing Fellows. (fun fact) [https://www.british-history.ac.uk/survey-london/vol38/pp220-227]
- Domed entrances to the Eastern and Western Galleries were built in 1891. [https://www.british-history.ac.uk/survey-london/vol38/pp220-227]
- The ten bells were cast by John Taylor of Loughborough. (fun fact) [https://www.british-history.ac.uk/survey-london/vol38/pp220-227]
- The later Royal College of Science building on the south side of the road began construction in 1900, so it must not appear in an 1893 view. [https://www.british-history.ac.uk/survey-london/vol38/pp233-247]
