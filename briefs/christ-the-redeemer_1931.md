## Summary
On 12 October 1931, Rio de Janeiro inaugurated the Christ the Redeemer statue atop Corcovado, a 30-metre Art Deco figure of reinforced concrete faced with soapstone. The monument was designed by Heitor da Silva Costa with sculptor Paul Landowski, and its head and hands were moulded in France and shipped to Brazil. The day's ceremonies included a camp mass at Estádio das Laranjeiras and a blessing by Cardinal Dom Sebastião Leme, with President Getúlio Vargas attending. The record for this year is patchy in English-language open sources; details of the inauguration time and lighting conflict, and some sources are available only as search snippets.

## Fun facts
- The statue is 30 metres tall on an 8-metre pedestal, with a 28-metre arm span and a weight of about 635 tonnes.
- It was designed by Heitor da Silva Costa with sculptor Paul Landowski; the face was by Gheorghe Leonida, and the head and hands were moulded in France.
- The monument cost 2,500 contos de réis, raised largely by Catholic donation campaigns, and is given as about US$250,000 in 1931.
- Guglielmo Marconi was to switch on the floodlights remotely from Rome, 9,200 km away, but bad weather meant the lights were activated on site.
- Before the statue, the summit had the iron-and-timber Chapéu do Sol belvedere of 1885, which was moved to a lower plateau to clear the site.
- Visitors reached the statue by the Corcovado Rack Railway, opened in 1884, or by the Paineiras road, and then climbed about 222 steps from the rail terminus.
