# Exhibition Road Reborn, 2011 (exhibition-road_2011)

**Status: DRAFT. A human must check every claim against its source before generation spends credits (H7).**

## Summary
In 2011 Exhibition Road was mostly a building site, then became a kerb-free shared surface. The Royal Borough of Kensington and Chelsea ran a 2003 design competition won by Dixon Jones, and the roughly £30 million scheme was completed on 8 December 2011. The finished road had a 20 mph limit and natural stone paving, and it was meant for some 11 to 12 million museum visits a year. The record for this exact 80-metre stretch during 2011 is thin, so the scene is reconstructed from the scheme's descriptions rather than a dated photo from the spot.

## Fun facts
- The road was about 800 m long and 25 m wide, serving the V&A, Science Museum, Natural History Museum and Imperial College.
- Construction lasted about three years and cost about £30 million; the borough records completion on 8 December 2011, on time and to budget.
- The new surface was pale granite in a diamond pattern on a dark ground, with cast-iron drainage channels and a tactile band about 4 m from the buildings.
- Lighting came from 26 masts about 20 m tall, set roughly every 25 m along the centre of the road.
- In the lower section, traffic kept to the east side and was separated mainly by benches and cycle racks.

## What the camera sees
Facing north up Exhibition Road in the finished state of late 2011: a continuous pale granite surface laid in a broad diamond or cross-hatch pattern on a dark ground, with no kerbs. On the left, the Science Museum's Exhibition Road frontage; on the right, the V&A's long Aston Webb range with its 1909 screen wall. Down the centre, tall thin lighting masts about 20 m high stand at intervals. Benches and cycle racks mark the edge of the carriageway on the right, where cars, black taxis and cycles move slowly. Pedestrians, museum visitors and students cross the shared surface freely.

Persists: The kerb-free shared granite surface with its pale diamond pattern on a dark ground.; The 20 mph speed limit and the central lighting masts.; The Science Museum frontage on the west and the V&A's Aston Webb range on the east.
