## Summary
In April 1941 bombs fell in Exhibition Road and badly wrecked the V&A's west side, blowing in the entrance doors, destroying the windows and iron grilles, and stripping much of the glass roofing on that front. The Aston Webb screen, built in 1909 to hide the boiler-house yard, was left chipped and cratered by blast. The stone scars were later kept deliberately and in 1987 an inscription by letter-cutter David Kindersley was cut around one of them. Sources differ over which raid caused which scars, so the damage may be cumulative.

## Fun facts
- The Aston Webb screen was built in 1909 as a solid stone wall with a colonnade above, designed to hide the museum's boiler-house yard.
- A 1941 damage report described two powerful bombs on Exhibition Road that 'practically wrecked the west side of the Museum' and blew in the Exhibition Road doors.
- Once the structural repairs were done, the blast holes were left on purpose; in 1985 an American visitor asked why the damage had been kept.
- David Kindersley preferred to cut an inscription around one of the bomb blasts rather than fix a tablet to the wall, and the work was finished in 1987.
- On the night of 10 May 1941 a bombed gas main burned in Exhibition Road, lighting the V&A, and the scene was painted that year.
- Between 1941 and 1944 the South Court served as a canteen, first for the RAF and later for bomb-damage repair squads.

## What the camera sees
Looking east across a kerbed London street at the V&A's western frontage on a wartime day. In the foreground a low, solid Portland-stone screen wall runs along the pavement, its surface freshly chipped and pocked with blast holes; above it a colonnade. The central entrance arch and doors are blown in and under repair, window openings are empty of glass, and the glass roofing on this side is largely missing. To the left the brick and terracotta Henry Cole Wing with its tower; to the right the main stone range running south. Overhead a pale, hazy sky with barrage-balloon cables.

### Script
Alfred Pike, museum porter, that's me. Mind the stonework as you come along, it's not what it was. Two bombs came down on Exhibition Road back in April and practically wrecked our west side; the doors were blown in and nearly every window and iron grille gone. The chipping on that screen wall is blast, and we've left it be for now. You'll want your gas mask on the shoulder and your torch shaded after dark, blackout's strict. Clothes are on coupons from June too, sixty-six for the year, so nobody's buying finery. Go careful along the pavement now. Goodbye, and mind how you go.
