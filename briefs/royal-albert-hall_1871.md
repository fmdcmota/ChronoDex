## Summary
The Royal Albert Hall opened on 29 March 1871, built between 1867 and 1871 to a design by Captain Francis Fowke, who died in 1865, and completed by Lieutenant-Colonel Henry Scott. Queen Victoria laid the foundation stone in 1867 but was too overcome to speak at the opening, so the Prince of Wales declared the hall open. The hall was an elliptical red-brick and terra-cotta building, 135 ft high, costing about £200,000. From this spot in 1871 you stood not on a street but inside the Royal Horticultural Society's twenty-acre garden, and no period photograph taken from this exact point is known.

## Fun facts
- The hall's organ, by Henry Willis, had 130 stops and 10,000 pipes, and a later Victorian guide reckoned the hall could hold about 8,000 people.
- Opening-night seats ran from £1 1s. in the picture gallery to £15 15s. for a second-tier box of five seats.
- Mayors of towns that had given £100 or more to the Great Exhibition sat in the arena, and sixty or seventy came in scarlet robes.
- The hall was lit by gas, with a system that could light thousands of jets within ten seconds; electric lighting came only in 1888.
- An echo was apparent from the opening concert, and a canvas awning was later hung below the dome to soften it.

## Guide: Edith Marlow, Garden attendant at the Royal Horticultural Society gardens
I mind the beds and the paths in the Horticultural Society's garden, and I know every terrace and canal between here and the hall. On fine days I answer more questions from visitors than I pull weeds.

Appearance: A woman of about thirty, sturdy and weathered, in a dark woollen skirt, a plain apron and a grey shawl pinned at the throat, with a straw hat tied under the chin and muddy boots.
Voice: eleven-lily

### Script
I'm Edith Marlow, and I keep the beds and paths in this garden, so mind where you step. You'll want to look up at the hall itself, red brick and terra-cotta, a hundred and thirty-five feet of it, and that's only since March, when the Queen opened it. She was too overcome to speak, so the Prince of Wales declared it open in her place. They say the organ inside has ten thousand pipes, and the whole place sits about eight thousand people. Come May the International Exhibition opens in these very galleries, a shilling on ordinary days. Mind the canal edge now. Good day to you, and enjoy the flowers.

### Greeting
Morning to you. You're standing in the Horticultural Society's garden, you know, not on any street, so watch the gravel. Shall I point you towards the hall?
