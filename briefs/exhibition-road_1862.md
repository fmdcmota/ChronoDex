# Exhibition Road Opens Beside the Great Show of 1862 (exhibition-road_1862)

## Summary
Exhibition Road was laid out in the late 1850s to commemorate the Great Exhibition of 1851, and in 1862 it was still a private road about five feet above the surrounding ground, not yet adopted by either parish. At its southern end stood Captain Francis Fowke's vast International Exhibition building, covering some 23½ acres and condemned by The Building News as "one of the ugliest public buildings that was ever raised in this country." The exhibition ran from 1 May to 1 November 1862 and drew over six million visitors, though the record is thin on the road's paving, lighting and shop life that year.

## Fun facts
- The 1862 exhibition building covered some 23½ acres and was put up with prefabricated timber, steam winches, travelling scaffolds and gas-flares at night to meet the opening date.
- A labourers' strike on the works was settled in January 1862 with a concession of an extra 6d. a day.
- The Building News called the design "one of the ugliest public buildings that was ever raised in this country."
- In 1859 anticipated ground rents on Exhibition Road were 35s. per foot of frontage, above Cromwell Road at 30s. but below Kensington Gore at £4.
- Visitors could see exhibits from 36 countries, among them the electric telegraph, William Morris decorative arts and an ice-making machine.
- St Margaret, Westminster, adopted its part of the road in 1863 and St Mary Abbots, Kensington, followed in 1867.

## Guide: Thomas Weller, Omnibus conductor on the Kensington Road
I've been a conductor on the London omnibuses for six years, and this spring the crowds for the great exhibition have kept me running up and down the stairs from morning till night. I know every stopping place and every regulation along Kensington Road, and I'll tell you plain what I see.

Appearance: A man of about thirty, lean and quick-moving, with a short beard; wears a dark frock coat, waistcoat, bowler hat and a conductor's ticket punch on a strap.
Voice: eleven-daniel

### Script
Thomas Weller, omnibus conductor, at your service. You've come at a busy time and no mistake. The exhibition opened the first of May and runs to the first of November, and over six million souls will pass through before it shuts. Sixpence a day extra the labourers won in January, after they struck on the works, and I'd say they earned it, with gas-flares burning all night to get the place open. They say it covers twenty-three and a half acres, and the Building News called it the ugliest public building ever raised here. Ugly or not, Cromwell Road is that congested we're forbidden to stop. Mind the step now. Good day!
